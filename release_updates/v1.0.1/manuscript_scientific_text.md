Introduction

Genomic antimicrobial-resistance prediction links an observed sequence to an expected phenotype. For Helicobacter pylori, substitutions in 23S rRNA and the quinolone-resistance-determining region of gyrA provide a well-established basis for interpreting clarithromycin and fluoroquinolone resistance [1-3]. Sequencing studies have used these associations to predict susceptibility and identify additional candidate variants [4-6]. These findings make genomic interpretation attractive, but using a catalogue in another setting requires two further conditions: the relevant sequence must be interpretable, and the relationship between the fixed rule and phenotype must remain sufficiently consistent.

Published cohorts have reported good or moderate agreement for selected drug–marker combinations [7-8]. Other studies have found inconsistent genotype–phenotype agreement [9] or incomplete characterization of resistance mechanisms for amoxicillin, metronidazole and rifampin [10]. Genome-wide multivariable predictors offer another approach [11], but model construction and external evaluation address different questions. High performance in a discovery cohort does not by itself establish performance in independently generated samples. Conversely, a poor external result does not show that an established mutation lacks biological relevance: it may reflect the catalogue, phenotype measurement, sequence product, isolate linkage or a combination of these factors.

Population structure is one plausible source of heterogeneity. H. pylori has extensive recombination and marked geographic differentiation [12-13], including regional variation within China [14-15]. We compared random partitions with clone-grouped and held-out-lineage partitions to examine sensitivity to the evaluation design. Yet population similarity cannot resolve every upstream uncertainty. An assembly that lacks the bases needed to interpret a resistance target has not demonstrated a susceptible genotype. Likewise, the genotype category accompanying a publication cannot substitute for an independent call from the exact sequence linked to the evaluation phenotype.

We therefore examined when fixed genomic rules remained interpretable after transfer, rather than developing another resistance predictor. We applied prespecified clarithromycin and levofloxacin catalogues to an international catalogue-development cohort and two independent Chinese cohorts with different sequence products and susceptibility-testing procedures. The primary evaluation separated target callability from performance among evaluable isolates. Secondary analyses examined the minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) of missed resistant isolates, agreement between callers and source-reported categories, population structure, and distance-based exclusion. Our aim was to characterize observed failure modes and the information needed to investigate them, while keeping clinical utility and causal explanations outside the scope of this retrospective study.

Methods

Study design, cohorts and analysis lock

This retrospective study reused public genomes and isolate-level susceptibility data. Catalogue definitions, phenotype authority, inclusion criteria, target-callability rules and the primary statistical criteria were frozen before cohort-wide external performance was examined. Pre-lock work was limited to literature review, linkage feasibility, phenotype harmonization and small technical feasibility checks that did not inspect cohort-wide external performance. The lock was internal rather than prospectively registered. Analyses added after the primary classifications are labelled secondary or explanatory below and could not alter a primary prediction or transportability classification.

HpGP provided the internal catalogue benchmark [6,13]: 414 of 419 phenotype identifiers had exact assembly links. The primary external cohort contained 60 Ningxia isolates with linked assemblies and Etest MICs [16]. The second external cohort contained 52 isolates with paired reads and study-defined susceptibility labels [17]. Its Methods describe disk diffusion, although its abstract states agar dilution; we retained the supplied labels rather than treating them as standardized MIC measurements. Published phenotype labels were the primary authority; intermediate or ambiguous labels were excluded from binary analyses. Exact MIC strings and inequality operators were retained. Both external cohorts were Chinese, and sequence product, study and assay were not independently varied. Cohort estimates were therefore reported separately, without attributing differences to geography or sequencing technology alone. Linkage, phenotype and quality-control details are provided in the Supplementary Methods under “Reproducibility and data lineage,” “Phenotype linkage and breakpoints,” “Assembly marker calls,” and “Genome quality and attrition,” and in Tables S1 and S2.

Fixed rules and target callability

The clarithromycin rule detected 23S rRNA A2142G, A2142C or A2143G. The levofloxacin rule detected gyrA A88V, A88P, N87K, N87I, D91G, D91N or D91Y. Both catalogues followed the HpGP resistance study [6]. No external phenotype was used to select variants. Common 23S mutation labels were reconciled with U27270.1 feature positions before calling.

Genome-level exclusions preceded diagnostic evaluation. Genome completeness and contamination were estimated with CheckM2 [18]. We applied basic assembly limits and study-prespecified thresholds of at least 90% completeness and no more than 10% contamination. SKESA support assemblies were used for the read cohort's genome-quality and population analyses, while read-based target calls remained authoritative [19]. A target had to span all required marker positions and meet the relevant identity, coverage, depth and allele-fraction criteria. For assemblies, these included 90% identity, explicit mapping of both 23S marker bases, and 90% query coverage for gyrA. Read calls required depth at least 10 and base/mapping quality at least 20; the pooled 23S resistant-allele threshold was 0.20. Complete criteria and the repeat/copy interpretation are provided in the Supplementary Methods under “Assembly marker calls” and “Raw-read calls.” Unrecoverable or ambiguous targets were unresolved, never recoded as susceptible.

External performance and error characterization

Fixed rules were applied without refitting. Sensitivity, specificity, false-susceptible rate (false-susceptible predictions divided by phenotypically resistant evaluable isolates), false-resistant rate, balanced accuracy and predictive values were calculated by cohort. Exact 95% binomial intervals accompanied proportions. An external cohort required at least 10 callable resistant and 10 callable susceptible isolates for the primary transportability classification. The 10% false-susceptible threshold and the other performance criteria were prespecified study criteria, not established clinical acceptance standards. Composite-metric uncertainty used 2,000 near-clone-group bootstrap replicates with seed 20260830; verified patient identifiers were unavailable.

Ningxia MIC comparisons retained censored values as lower bounds. We examined error direction, MIC distributions and the proportion of missed resistant isolates above selected MIC thresholds rather than estimating treatment outcomes. A multicentre H. pylori study found high categorical agreement between Etest and agar dilution after discrepancy testing, alongside interlaboratory and method-related variation [20]. That evidence does not quantify assay error in our cohorts or explain their genotype-phenotype discrepancies. Source-reported genotype categories were compared with independently recalled calls but never substituted for them. A second caller derived gyrA substitutions from whole-genome alignments. Agreement between two callers tests reproducibility from the same deposited assemblies; it does not verify the originally phenotyped specimen or its sequence-phenotype linkage. For transparency, a per-isolate audit joined sample identifiers, versioned assembly accessions, acquisition checksums, phenotypes, source-reported categories, both independent recalls and discrepancy classes. No provenance field was used to change a prediction or phenotype.

Relatedness, population structure and robustness

Sequence identifiers were checked for overlap. We calculated Mash distance [21] and skani nucleotide identity and bidirectional aligned fractions [22]. Near-clone groups had to meet the study-prespecified thresholds for all three measures. A core-SNP representation and eight computational clusters were fitted in HpGP alone; external genomes were projected without refitting. These clusters are not formal named populations. Primary robustness analyses compared random-isolate, clone-grouped and held-out-lineage evaluation, and included phenotype reinterpretation, clone thinning, read/assembly concordance, random-marker controls and training-label permutations. Full parameters and eligible denominators are provided in the Supplementary Methods under “Near-clone grouping,” “Core-SNP clusters,” “Bootstrap and intervals,” “Random-panel and label-permutation controls,” and the associated robustness subsections.

For the validation-design comparisons in Figure 5a,b, we fitted prespecified auxiliary L2-penalized logistic regressions (C=1, liblinear, probability threshold 0.5) using mutation-only, lineage-only or mutation-plus-lineage feature sets. Each model was estimated only in its training partition and then applied to the corresponding held-out or external data. These trained models were diagnostic probes of validation design and information content. They were distinct from the primary deterministic fixed catalogue, did not redefine any marker rule, and could not change any external fixed-rule prediction or transportability classification.

Secondary analyses, conducted after the primary classifications were finalized, examined 23S alignment sensitivity, assembly-quality associations, full-gyrA missense variants, an add-only N87Y scenario, leave-one-isolate-out stability and predictive-value scenarios. Population analyses quantified five-nearest-HpGP distance, phenotype-blind cohort discrimination, within-cluster performance and direct lineage standardization. A further coverage analysis included every phenotype-linked binary isolate, retaining QC failures and uncallable targets as unresolved. Logical sensitivity and specificity bounds assigned unresolved isolates to the least or most favourable compatible predictions; they are not confidence intervals. Distance-based exclusion used fixed percentiles of the HpGP distance distribution without selecting a cutoff on external phenotypes. External-cohort differences used Newcombe intervals and Fisher exact tests. These analyses explain or test the stability of the primary findings; they were not all part of the original analysis lock.

Generative AI assistance

OpenAI ChatGPT and Codex were used under author direction from 30 August to 7 September 2026 to assist with code refactoring, unit-test drafting, documentation, consistency checks and figure-layout scripting. The authors specified the scientific questions, input data, frozen rules and statistical criteria; executed and reviewed the workflows; and verified the numerical outputs and interpretations. No generative-AI tool generated or altered primary measurements, phenotype labels or fixed-rule predictions.

Results

Prespecified design separated target availability from external performance

Exact phenotype-to-sequence linkage produced 526 public genomes: 414 HpGP assemblies, 60 Ningxia assemblies and 52 paired-read datasets with support assemblies. After the prespecified basic and CheckM2 criteria were applied, 517 genomes remained: 414/414 HpGP, 57/60 Ningxia and 46/52 read-cohort genomes (Figure 1a-c; Table S1). These genome-QC counts are distinct from drug-specific evaluable denominators, which additionally require a binary phenotype and a callable target. Both targets were callable in all HpGP assemblies. In Ningxia, gyrA was callable in 57/60 linked isolates but a marker-spanning 23S rRNA target was recoverable in only 6/60. In the read cohort, 23S rRNA and gyrA were callable in 46/52 and 42/52 linked isolates before ambiguous phenotypes were excluded (Figure 1d-g; Table S2).

The three cohorts represented different evaluation roles rather than interchangeable samples. HpGP was the internal catalogue benchmark; the Ningxia assembly cohort provided exact Etest MICs; and the paired-read cohort provided study-defined disk-diffusion phenotypes. Catalogue variants, phenotype authority, target criteria and primary performance rules were held fixed. Both external cohorts were Chinese, and study, sequence product and susceptibility procedure varied together, so their contrast was reported without attributing it to geography or sequencing technology alone.

Fixed catalogues showed divergent performance in the two external cohorts

The fixed catalogues had high internal performance in HpGP. The clarithromycin rule correctly classified all 108 resistant and 306 susceptible isolates. The levofloxacin rule classified 131/133 resistant and 281/281 susceptible isolates, corresponding to sensitivity of 98.5% and specificity of 100% (Figure 2; Tables S3 and S4). These values describe the catalogue benchmark and were not treated as independent validation.

Performance in the paired-read external cohort was good but imperfect. Clarithromycin sensitivity was 90.0% (95% CI 68.3-98.8) and specificity 95.8% (78.9-99.9) among 44 evaluable isolates. Levofloxacin sensitivity was 87.5% (61.7-98.4) and specificity 95.7% (78.1-99.9) among 39 evaluable isolates. The corresponding balanced accuracies were 92.9% and 91.6%, and the false-susceptible rates were 10.0% and 12.5%.

Ningxia clarithromycin did not meet the prespecified minimum evaluable denominator and therefore did not yield a cohort-level performance estimate. Ningxia levofloxacin was evaluable in 57 final-QC isolates but showed sensitivity of 42.9% (24.5-62.8), specificity of 75.9% (56.5-89.7), balanced accuracy of 59.4% and a false-susceptible rate of 57.1% (Figure 2). Sensitivity was 44.6 percentage points lower than in the paired-read cohort (95% difference interval -63.3 to -15.0; P=0.0046; Table S5d). Because the external settings were heterogeneous, a pooled estimate would conceal the main result.

Incomplete 23S recovery left most Ningxia clarithromycin results unresolved

A marker-spanning 23S rRNA call met the interpretation and genome-QC criteria in only 6/60 Ningxia isolates. The other 54 were unresolved, not susceptible (Figure 3a,b). Target screening identified a marker-spanning segment in seven assemblies, one of which failed genome QC; 53 lacked the required span. All six evaluable isolates were classified correctly, but they comprised only two resistant and four susceptible isolates and could not establish cohort-level performance. By comparison, 23S rRNA was callable in all HpGP isolates and in 46/52 read-cohort isolates before exclusion of ambiguous phenotypes.

Ordinary whole-assembly metrics did not distinguish the six callable from the 54 uncallable Ningxia assemblies: all comparisons of contig count, log10 N50, CheckM2 completeness and contamination had P values of at least 0.199 (Supplementary Figure S1; Table S6). The secondary BLAST task, identity and coverage grid retained six final-QC callable assemblies under every tested setting. Most other assemblies had partial 23S-like matches without the required marker-spanning context, and no additional final-QC assembly was rescued by relaxing these alignment settings (Supplementary Figure S2; Tables S7a,b). Without source-matched raw reads, these observations support inadequate target representation in the available assemblies but do not identify the mechanism by which it arose.

When every phenotype-linked binary isolate was retained, Ningxia yielded a correct clarithromycin result for 6/60 (10.0%). The 29 unresolved resistant and 25 unresolved susceptible isolates left full-cohort sensitivity logically compatible with values from 6.5% to 100% and specificity from 13.8% to 100% (Figure 3c; Tables S5a,b). These are deterministic identification ranges, not confidence intervals around the six-isolate subset. A marker-negative result therefore requires that the relevant target bases were actually callable; target non-recovery remains an unresolved result (Figure 3d).

Levofloxacin false-susceptible calls included isolates with high MICs

Unlike the clarithromycin target, Ningxia gyrA was evaluable in all 57 final-QC isolates. The fixed rule nevertheless missed 16/28 phenotypically resistant isolates, producing 12 true-resistant, 22 true-susceptible, seven false-resistant and 16 false-susceptible classifications (Figure 4a). Eleven of the 16 missed resistant isolates had reported MIC lower bounds of at least 8 mg/L; the median lower-bound MIC was 16 mg/L (interquartile range 3.5-32), and six values were right-censored above 32 mg/L (Figure 4b,c; Tables S8 and S9d). The errors were therefore not confined to isolates near the breakpoint. Treatment outcomes were unavailable, so these findings indicate potentially consequential error direction rather than observed eradication failure.

The primary result was not driven by a single influential isolate. Removing each of the 57 isolates in turn left the false-susceptible rate between 55.6% and 59.3% (Figure 4d; Table S10b). Near-clone-group bootstraps preserved the contrast with the read cohort while showing the uncertainty expected from modest external denominators, and predictive-value scenarios showed that the lower Ningxia sensitivity progressively reduced the implied negative predictive value as assumed resistance prevalence increased (Supplementary Figure S3; Tables S10a,c). These curves are scenarios rather than calibration in an additional population.

Sequence reproducibility and sequence-phenotype provenance gave different answers. Targeted-alignment and whole-genome-alignment callers agreed on all 57 deposited assemblies, whereas independently recalled and source-reported catalogue categories agreed for 31/57 (54.4%) and differed for 26/57. One difference, SHZY43, was attributable to catalogue scope: both independent callers identified N87Y, which the source rule classified as resistance-associated [16] but the frozen study catalogue excluded. The remaining 25 differences comprised 16 source-reported resistant/frozen-panel susceptible and nine source-reported susceptible/frozen-panel resistant categories (Figure 4e,f; Table S11; Table S12). The audit links every isolate to its accession, sequence checksum, phenotype record, source category and both recalls, and explicitly separates the rule-definition-explained row from unresolved provenance discordances. The remaining 25 cannot be assigned to sequence-version differences, isolate linkage, within-isolate variation, source genotyping or another pipeline explanation from the available public material. Source-reported categories were retained as audit comparators and were never substituted for independent sequence calls.

Among the 16 false-susceptible predictions, 15 isolates were wild type at the prespecified quinolone-resistance-determining-region positions under both callers; SHZY43 carried off-panel N87Y. Because N87Y was excluded from the frozen catalogue, the primary classification remained false susceptible. Adding N87Y in an exploratory scenario recovered only that isolate and reduced the false-susceptible rate to 53.6%. No full-gyrA missense variant passed false-discovery correction (minimum adjusted P=0.313; Supplementary Figures S4 and S5; Tables S13a-c). These analyses did not identify a new resistance mechanism and cannot exclude structural, regulatory, heteroresistant or non-gyrA explanations.

Validation architecture and phenotype-blind controls supported a genuine external contrast

Full Mash/skani screening identified four two-isolate near-clone components, all confined to HpGP; no near clone crossed a development/external boundary (Supplementary Figure S6; Tables S14a,b). Figure 5a,b reports auxiliary logistic-regression comparisons used to probe validation design rather than the primary deterministic fixed-rule estimates. For the mutation-only auxiliary clarithromycin model, mean balanced accuracy was 1.000 under random-isolate and clone-grouped internal evaluation, 0.957 under leave-lineage-out evaluation and 0.929 in the estimable external read cohort. For levofloxacin, the corresponding internal values were 0.980, 0.954 and 0.990, whereas the external estimates were 0.594 in Ningxia and 0.916 in the read cohort (Figure 5a,b; Tables S15a,b). These trained models tested whether internal validation design anticipated external behaviour; they neither refitted nor replaced the fixed catalogue.

Within the same auxiliary modelling framework, phenotype-blind, size- and prevalence-matched random-SNP panels yielded mean probability AUCs near 0.50, and training-label permutations likewise remained near chance (Figure 5c,d; Tables S16a-d). Lineage-only auxiliary models generally predicted the majority susceptible class and did not reproduce the mutation-catalogue signal. Near-clone thinning did not change any primary external fixed-rule conclusion. For the paired-read cohort, the primary read calls and support-assembly calls were concordant whenever both were callable: 44/44 clarithromycin and 41/41 levofloxacin isolates (Figure 5e,f).

These checks do not prove that the external phenotype labels, deposited assemblies or catalogue are error free. They address narrower alternatives: close-relative leakage did not account for the high internal benchmark; arbitrary frequency-matched SNP panels and permuted training labels did not recreate the signal; and read-versus-support-assembly implementation did not explain the better-performing paired-read cohort. The remaining Ningxia discrepancy therefore required separate investigation of target availability, marker-phenotype agreement and provenance rather than a single accuracy summary.

Population context and marker-phenotype architecture did not rescue the Ningxia deficit

Eight computational clusters were fitted in HpGP and external genomes were projected without refitting. The external cohorts projected mainly to cluster 3: 52/57 Ningxia and 44/46 read-cohort final-QC genomes. Both cohorts had nearby HpGP neighbours in the development representation, and incorrect Ningxia levofloxacin calls were no farther from HpGP than correct calls (P=0.968; Cliff's delta 0.008; Figure 6a,b; Tables S17a,b). Nevertheless, phenotype-blind core-SNP components distinguished HpGP from Ningxia and from the read cohort, with out-of-fold AUCs of 0.899 and 0.918, respectively (both permutation P=0.001; Figure 6c; Supplementary Figure S7). Local proximity and detectable study-level genomic shift therefore coexisted.

Within dominant cluster 3, Ningxia levofloxacin sensitivity remained 12/23 (52.2%) versus 22/22 (100%) in HpGP (P=0.00020), and specificity was 22/29 (75.9%) versus 38/38 (100%; P=0.0018; Figure 6d; Supplementary Figure S8). Directly standardizing HpGP to Ningxia's phenotype-specific cluster frequencies retained expected sensitivity and specificity of 100% rather than reproducing the observed deficit (Figure 6e). These are study-conditioned comparisons within computational strata; lineage composition was not established as a causal explanation.

Marker architecture further localized the mismatch without resolving it. Fixed levofloxacin markers tracked phenotypic resistance closely in HpGP and the read cohort, with marker-minus-phenotype prevalence gaps of -0.5 and -2.6 percentage points, but underestimated Ningxia resistance by 15.8 points. All 16 Ningxia false-susceptible isolates were marker wild type under the frozen rule, whereas six of seven false-resistant isolates carried N87K. The discrepancy was concentrated in cluster 3, but sparse external membership in other clusters prevented separation of study and lineage effects (Supplementary Figure S9; Tables S18a,b and S9a-c).

Phenotype-blind exclusion based on fixed development-distance percentiles also failed to identify a reliable subset. Every eligible Ningxia levofloxacin subset exceeded the prespecified 10% false-susceptible threshold; the lowest rate was 50.0% while retaining 50/57 primary isolates (87.7% coverage; Figure 6f; Supplementary Figure S10; Table S5c). This result concerns the tested distance representation and cutoffs, not every possible abstention method.

Coverage-aware synthesis preserved uncertainty instead of converting it into susceptibility

Under the prespecified criteria, clarithromycin had insufficient cross-cohort evidence because only one external cohort met the minimum requirement of at least 10 callable resistant and 10 callable susceptible isolates. Levofloxacin was classified as having high false-susceptible risk because the Ningxia point estimate exceeded the prespecified threshold (Table S19). Phenotype reinterpretation, clone thinning and quality-stratified sensitivity analyses did not change these primary classifications (Supplementary Figure S11; Tables S20a,b and S16a-c). A grid of minimum denominators and false-susceptible thresholds showed which choices changed the labels, but it was an explicit sensitivity analysis rather than an externally validated acceptance rule (Supplementary Figure S12; Table S10d).

Coverage-aware yield placed performance estimates back in the intended cohort denominator. Ningxia produced a correct clarithromycin result for 6/60 linked binary isolates and a correct levofloxacin result for 34/60. The corresponding yields were 41/50 and 36/48 in the paired-read cohort (Table 1). Performance among evaluable isolates and correct-result yield therefore answer different questions: the former asks how the rule behaved when interpretable, whereas the latter asks how often the complete workflow returned a correct result for the linked target population.

The two external levofloxacin settings were formally heterogeneous. In addition to the 44.6-point sensitivity difference, the Ningxia-minus-read false-susceptible-rate difference was +44.6 points (95% Newcombe interval 15.0-63.3), and the balanced-accuracy difference was -32.2 points (95% group-bootstrap interval -47.1 to -17.4; Table S5d). Group-bootstrap and leave-one-out analyses preserved the direction of this contrast (Supplementary Figure S3). These results support cohort-specific reporting and a reporting chain that keeps target callability, fixed-rule agreement, sequence provenance and error direction separate.

Discussion

This study identified two non-equivalent limits to transferring fixed H. pylori resistance rules. Clarithromycin interpretation was mostly unavailable in the Ningxia assembly cohort because the required 23S sequence could not be recovered. Levofloxacin targets were recoverable, but the frozen rule missed more than half of the phenotypically resistant evaluable isolates, including isolates with high MIC lower bounds. Better performance in the independent paired-read cohort shows that the finding is context dependent rather than a general rejection of genomic prediction. The contribution is to separate target availability, fixed-rule disagreement and unresolved provenance, to show the validation architecture supporting the contrast, and to test whether simple population-distance or marker extensions resolve it without changing the rule in response to external outcomes.

Earlier genotype-phenotype studies established important biological associations and useful prediction results [4,6]. Our findings do not invalidate 23S rRNA or gyrA as resistance determinants. A mutation can contribute to resistance while a catalogue incorporating it performs inconsistently when sequence products, linked phenotypes and bacterial backgrounds change. Published agreement studies have likewise reported variable results for the drug–marker combinations examined [7-9], with additional gaps reported for amoxicillin, metronidazole and rifampin [10]. The present study extends those comparisons by evaluating target information, internal-validation architecture, null controls, sequence-provenance reproducibility, population context and the MIC distribution of missed resistant isolates alongside fixed-rule performance.

Callability is upstream of classification. Failure to recover both 23S marker bases is not evidence that those bases are wild type. The correctly classified six-isolate Ningxia subset illustrates the problem with reporting only evaluable-subset accuracy: it leaves most of the intended cohort uninterpreted. Broadly relaxing alignment settings did not increase recovery, but the missing original reads prevent attribution to a specific sequencing or assembly mechanism. In genomic interpretation, uncertainty should not be converted into susceptibility. The novelty here is not that logical principle alone, but its empirical scale, its effect on correct-result yield and the demonstration that ordinary whole-assembly quality summaries did not flag the target-level problem.

For levofloxacin, the direction of error matters because a susceptible prediction could influence selection of a drug to which an isolate is phenotypically resistant. The missed Ningxia isolates were not confined to the breakpoint margin, and the high false-susceptible rate remained after every leave-one-isolate-out analysis. Summary accuracy alone would understate this risk. At the same time, treatment regimens, host factors and outcomes were unavailable, so the study does not estimate eradication failure, adverse events or clinical benefit. The 10% threshold is an explicit benchmark choice and should not be presented as a universal clinical tolerance.

The validation controls narrow several alternative explanations. No near clone crossed a development/external boundary; clone-grouped and held-out-lineage internal estimates remained high; frequency-matched random-SNP panels and training-label permutations stayed near chance; and raw-read versus support-assembly calls agreed in the paired-read cohort when both were callable. The auxiliary logistic regressions in Figure 5 were trained only to compare validation designs and feature sets; they were not a refit of the deterministic fixed catalogue and did not alter the primary external predictions. These results support the reality of the external contrast as an evaluation finding, but they do not identify its causal source. In particular, strong internal validation cannot certify transportability, and phenotype-blind controls cannot verify the external sequence-phenotype linkage.

Population analyses likewise support a bounded conclusion. Both external cohorts were genomically distinguishable from HpGP, but Ningxia errors were not confined to remote points in the development representation. Performance remained lower within the shared dominant cluster and after standardization, while the tested distance-based exclusions did not recover a subset meeting the study criteria. Marginal cluster composition and simple geometric distance were therefore insufficient explanations or safeguards in these data. They were not ruled out as contributors, and study, assay, sequence snapshot and computational cluster remained partly confounded.

Marker-prevalence analyses showed that the fixed panel approximately tracked phenotype prevalence in HpGP and the paired-read cohort but underestimated Ningxia levofloxacin resistance. This helps describe where the rule-phenotype relationship changed, not why it changed. The disagreement with source-reported genotype categories is especially important: two callers agreeing on deposited assemblies establishes reproducibility from those assemblies, not identity with the biological material originally phenotyped. Of the 26 category differences, one (SHZY43) is explained by catalogue scope because the source rule included N87Y [16] and the frozen study catalogue did not. The revised provenance worksheet separates this row from 25 unresolved source-recall differences and makes the accession, checksum, phenotype and both recall results explicit for every linked Ningxia isolate. Source-matched raw reads and repeat phenotyping are still needed to distinguish sequence-version, isolate-linkage, within-isolate, source-genotyping and biological explanations for those 25 rows. The unresolved discrepancies should not be interpreted as evidence of misconduct or error by the original investigators.

Retraining a classifier on the Ningxia outcomes would answer a different question and consume the independence required to evaluate the fixed catalogue. The off-panel N87Y scenario and full-gyrA scan were retained as explanatory analyses, but neither justified post hoc catalogue replacement or established a new mechanism. Mechanistic experiments could clarify particular discordances, yet they would not retrospectively establish portability of the original rules. The most direct next validation would combine version-matched raw reads, repeat susceptibility testing and targeted sequencing of discordant isolates, followed by testing in additional independently assembled cohorts. A prespecified read-downsampling experiment in a source-matched cohort could characterize how declining sequence information affects callability and unresolved-result rates, but it would not by itself explain the Ningxia assemblies.

Several limitations constrain generalization. Both external cohorts were Chinese and had modest sample sizes with wide intervals. The paired-read cohort used study-defined disk labels, including overlapping levofloxacin endpoint descriptions, rather than directly comparable MICs. Original Ningxia reads and verified patient identifiers were unavailable. HpGP was an internal catalogue benchmark, not independent clinical validation. Secondary analyses were conducted after the primary results and should be interpreted as explanatory rather than prospectively confirmatory. The logical bounds make no assumption about unresolved results, and the distance cutoffs test one particular abstention strategy. No reculture, repeat susceptibility testing, prospective implementation or treatment-outcome validation was performed.

The results support separating target interpretability, rule-phenotype agreement, validation architecture and sequence provenance when genomic rules are evaluated externally. Reports should preserve unresolved outcomes, give cohort-specific denominators and uncertainty, document version-matched sequence-phenotype records and examine clinically consequential error directions. For the tested catalogues and public data products, clarithromycin lacked sufficient cross-cohort evidence and levofloxacin showed a high false-susceptible rate in one external setting. These findings define limits of the evaluated rules and data products; they neither certify nor reject genomic resistance prediction as a clinical strategy in general.
